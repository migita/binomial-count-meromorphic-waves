\begin{abstract}
We study meromorphic travelling waves of
\(u_t+uu_x+\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_j\partial_x^{j+1}u=0\), a
constant-coefficient family containing KdV--Burgers,
Kuramoto--Sivashinsky and Kawahara. After integration and
normalization, the profiles satisfy \(P(D)v+v^2/2=0\), where
\(D=\mathrm d/\mathrm dz\) and \(P\) is monic of degree \(p\ge2\) with
complex coefficients.

If \(p\) is odd or \(P\) contains only even powers, we give a complete
algebraic description of all nonconstant globally meromorphic
solutions: they are rational, rational in an exponential, or elliptic,
with one pole modulo their full period group.

We then count normalized one-pole pairs \((P,v)\) across all monic \(P\),
whether or not \(P\) is covered by the classification. When \(2p-1\) is
prime, there are \(\binom{2p-1}{p-1}\) rational-exponential pairs at a
fixed exponential rate and \(2\binom{2p-2}{p-2}\) elliptic pairs on a
fixed generic lattice, all of multiplicity one. Exact computer
certificates establish both formulas with multiplicities for
\(2\le p\le73\). We conjecture the same counting and multiplicity
statements in every order.

Inside the reflection-symmetric families, which contain the
KdV--Kawahara hierarchy and the Kuramoto--Sivashinsky equation without
dispersion, the rational-exponential count is
\(\binom{p-1}{\lfloor(p-1)/2\rfloor}\). For a fixed evolution
equation, the enumeration shows that such waves exist only on finitely
many scaling curves in the space of coefficients.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}
Meromorphic travelling waves; Laurent series; elliptic functions;
polynomial convolution; algebraic multiplicity.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The generalized Kuramoto--Sivashinsky equation has nonconstant travelling waves
that are polynomials in a hyperbolic tangent at exactly four values of
its squared dimensionless dispersion parameter. Kuramoto and Tsuzuki
found the dispersionless one \citep{KuramotoTsuzuki1976}, Kudryashov the
other three \citep{Kudryashov1988,Kudryashov1990}, and Eremenko proved
that, together with Kudryashov's elliptic solution and a single rational
one, they exhaust the nonconstant meromorphic travelling waves
\citep{Eremenko2006}.
Counted as we count below, the tanh list has ten members. This paper
shows that ten is \(\binom52\): the classical list is the order-three
case of a binomial enumeration, proved for infinitely many orders and
conjectured for all.

We consider the evolution equations
\[
 u_t+uu_x+\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_j\partial_x^{j+1}u=0,\qquad b_p\ne0,
\]
which contain KdV--Burgers (\(p=2\)), Kuramoto--Sivashinsky (\(p=3\))
and Kawahara (\(p=4\)), the sixth-order turbulence models of
\citet{KudryashovMigita2007,SimbawaMatthewsCox2010}, and the quadratic
long-wave reductions of \citet{KudryashovSinelshchikovChernyavsky2008}.
A travelling wave \(u=w(x-ct)\) satisfies, after one integration,
\(\sum_jb_jw^{(j)}-cw+w^2/2+K=0\). Shifting \(w\) by a constant
equilibrium and normalizing the leading coefficient gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 E_P(v):=P(D)v+\frac12v^2=0,\qquad
 P(\rho)=\rho^p+\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}a_j\rho^j,\qquad p\ge2,
\end{equation}
with \(D=\mathrm d/\mathrm dz\) and complex coefficients. The speed,
the integration constant and the equilibrium are absorbed into
\(a_0\). Throughout, \(p\) is the order of \eqref{eq:main}, one less
than the order of the evolution equation.

\paragraph{Completeness.}
Eremenko's theorem states that an autonomous algebraic differential
equation with a single monomial of top degree and finitely many Laurent
expansions at a pole has only three kinds of nonconstant meromorphic
solutions: rational, rational in an exponential, and elliptic
\citep{Eremenko2006}. Theorem~\ref{thm:complete} verifies these
hypotheses directly for \eqref{eq:main} when \(p\) is odd, and applies
the theorem to a fixed-energy equation when \(P\) is an even polynomial.
Every nonconstant meromorphic solution is then of one of the three
kinds and has a single pole modulo its full period group, and for
fixed \(P\) there is at most one nonconstant solution up to translation
when \(p\) is odd and at most one on each energy level when \(P\) is
even. Section~\ref{sec:systems} writes each kind as a finite
polynomial system, following the principal-part construction of
\citet{DeminaKudryashov2011,DeminaKudryashovElliptic2011}. These systems
therefore give an exhaustive representation of the nonconstant
meromorphic travelling waves in the covered cases. The matching
systems are valid for every monic \(P\), and the counts below range
over the full monic family; parity enters only through exhaustion.

\paragraph{The count.}
Fix the exponential rate to one and place the pole at \(i\pi\).
Every solution of \eqref{eq:main} that is rational in \(e^z\), has
one pole modulo \(2\pi i\ZZ\) and vanishes as \(e^z\to0\) is of the
form \(v=s_pA(D)Q\), where \(Q=1/(1+e^{-z})\), \(s_p\) is a fixed
scale and \(A\) is a monic polynomial of degree \(p-1\).
Let \(P\) range over all monic polynomials of degree \(p\) and count
the pairs \((P,v)\) for which \(v\) solves \eqref{eq:main}.
Likewise fix a nonsingular Weierstrass lattice and count the pairs
\((P,v)\) with \(v\) elliptic on that lattice and its sole pole at
zero. We prove
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 N_p&=\binom{2p-1}{p-1}
     &&\text{rational-exponential pairs},\\
 M_p&=2\binom{2p-2}{p-2}
     &&\text{elliptic pairs on a generic lattice}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Whenever \(2p-1\) is prime, both formulas hold and every pair is
simple, that is, nondegenerate; in the elliptic case this is for a
generic lattice (Theorems~\ref{thm:prime} and~\ref{thm:elliptic-count}).
For every \(2\le p\le73\), the formulas hold as counts with algebraic
multiplicity (Section~\ref{sec:counting}).
At \(p=5\), where \(2p-1=9\) is the first composite index, a separate
exact computation shows that all \(126\) rational-exponential pairs
are simple.
We conjecture the same counting and simplicity statements in every
order (Conjecture~\ref{conj:count}). The count is over operators,
not over the waves of a single equation; Section~\ref{sec:fixed}
reads it for a single equation.

For rational-exponential profiles, the pair \((P,v_A)\) solves
\eqref{eq:main} if and only if \(A\) divides its own discrete
self-convolution \(S_A(n)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}A(j)A(n-j)\), and then
\(P=s_pS_A/(2A)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:divisibility}). When
\(\ell=2p-1\) is prime, \(\ell S_A\equiv\pm(\rho^\ell-\rho)\pmod\ell\),
so the condition reduces to \(A\mid\rho^\ell-\rho\) over \(\FF_\ell\).
The monic divisors of degree \(p-1\) are indexed by the
\((p-1)\)-element subsets of \(\FF_\ell\).
There are \(\binom{\ell}{p-1}\) of them, and each lifts by Hensel's
lemma to a simple \(\ell\)-adic solution. A weighted B\'ezout bound
shows that the lifts account for the whole solution scheme, and
finiteness over \(\QQ\) transfers the count to \(\CC\). For the
elliptic pairs, the same weighted B\'ezout argument gives the count
with multiplicity on every nonsingular lattice, once a cusp
degeneration excludes solutions at infinity; degenerating the lattice
to a node instead, where the elliptic problem becomes the
equal-endpoint case of the exponential one with two choices of
background, proves that the pairs are simple on a generic lattice.
Two ingredients resist a uniform proof. One is a rigidity statement
for the continuous convolution \(\int_0^\rho A(t)A(\rho-t)\dd t\),
needed to exclude solutions at infinity; it is proved for prime and
prime-power indices, certified by exact arithmetic through degree
\(72\), and would give both formulas with multiplicity in every order.
The other is simplicity of every rational-exponential pair, proved
for prime indices and, by exact computation, at \(p=5\), and open in
general (Conjecture~\ref{conj:count}).

\paragraph{Symmetric families and fixed equations.}
Two refinements connect the count with individual equations.
The reflection \(z\mapsto-z\) acts on the normalized pairs, and its
fixed points are the pairs whose operator is even, for even \(p\), or
has an odd nonconstant part, for odd \(p\). These are the evolution
equations with only odd-order derivatives, such as KdV and Kawahara,
and those whose linear part has only even-order derivatives, such as
Kuramoto--Sivashinsky without dispersion. Inside these families the
number of rational-exponential pairs is
\[
 F_p=\binom{p-1}{\lfloor(p-1)/2\rfloor},
\]
under the same hypotheses as for \(N_p\), and for even \(p\) the even
family has \(2F_p\) elliptic pairs on a generic lattice
(Theorem~\ref{thm:symmetric} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:elliptic-symmetric}). The Kawahara counts \(3\) and
\(6\) are \(F_4\) and \(2F_4\), and at \(p=5\) the six symmetric pairs
contain the four kink solutions of \citet{KudryashovMigita2007}.

For a fixed evolution equation, rescaling the rate moves the operator
along a weighted curve in the space of coefficients. The equation has a
one-pole travelling wave rational in an exponential if and only if its
coefficient vector lies on one of at most \((N_p+F_p)/2\) such curves,
and then it has finitely many (Corollary~\ref{cor:solvable}). For odd
\(5\le p\le73\), the equations with any nonconstant meromorphic
travelling wave form a set of dimension at most two in the
\((p-1)\)-dimensional space of coefficients, so a generic equation has
none (Corollary~\ref{cor:generic}). At \(p=3\) this set is the union of
the four classical Kuramoto--Sivashinsky curves.

\paragraph{Relation to earlier work.}
The completeness theorem is a uniform version of results that exist in
pieces. For odd \(p\) the indicial polynomial has no positive integer
zero, the Laurent expansion at a pole is unique, and Eremenko's theorem
applies directly; this case is contained in
\citet[Theorem~1]{YuanLiQi2014}, with related odd-order results in
\citet{HuangZhangChenYuan2014,YuanWuChenHuang2014} and a fifth-order
instance in \citet{Dang2023Odd}. For even \(p\) the index \(3p\) is
resonant, and the conclusion need not hold for mixed operators: the
Fisher equation has an elliptic-exponential family of solutions
\citep{AblowitzZeppetella1979,Kudryashov2010,ConteNgWu2015} whose
generic members are meromorphic and of none of the three kinds.
The familiar Ablowitz--Zeppetella front is a rational-exponential
degeneration of this family.
When \(P\) is an even polynomial the equation has a first integral,
the energy, and fixing its value removes the resonant freedom.
This is the device of
\citet[Lemmas~3--4]{EremenkoLiaoNg2009} for \(P=D^p+a_0\), stated as a
general step by \citet{ConteNgWu2018,ConteMusetteNgWu2025} and applied
to the Kawahara equation \citep{DeminaKudryashovKawahara2010,YeEtAl2022},
the generalized Bretherton equation
\citep{DeminaKudryashov2011,KudryashovSinelshchikovDemina2011} and the
seventh-order KdV equation \citep{Dang2023Even}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:complete} extends it uniformly to every even \(P\),
intermediate derivatives included. Equations with two dominant
monomials, or without the finiteness property, are treated by other
methods in \citet{Demina2019,Demina2022}.
The general enumeration has no precedent that we know of; its special
cases at \(p=3\) and in the even Kawahara family are classical, as
the examples make explicit.

\paragraph{Examples.}
The rational-exponential and generic elliptic counts are, respectively,
\(3\) and \(2\) for KdV--Burgers, \(10\) and \(8\) for
Kuramoto--Sivashinsky, and \(3\) and \(6\) for the Kawahara family,
which restricts \(P\) to even polynomials, against \(35\) and \(30\)
for the unrestricted order-four family.
The ten Kuramoto--Sivashinsky pairs are
the classical list tabulated by \citet{KudryashovZargaryan1996} and
\citet{ConteMusette2009}; the elliptic solution and its parameter
condition are due to \citet{Kudryashov1990}, with necessity proved by
\citet{Hone2005} and \citet{Eremenko2006}; the three Kawahara values
are those of \citet{DeminaKudryashovKawahara2010}. The
Kuramoto--Sivashinsky example also exhibits the two exceptional values
\(j=0\) and \(j=-300\) of the lattice invariant at which elliptic pairs
collide. All counts are over \(\CC\); the examples identify the real
profiles, and stability is not discussed. Section~\ref{sec:open}
collects the open questions, and Appendix~\ref{app:verification}
describes the exact verification and the ancillary material.
