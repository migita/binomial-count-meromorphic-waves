\begin{abstract}
We study meromorphic travelling waves of
\(u_t+uu_x+\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_j\partial_x^{j+1}u=0\), a
constant-coefficient family containing KdV--Burgers,
Kuramoto--Sivashinsky and Kawahara. After integration and
normalization, the profiles satisfy \(P(D)v+v^2/2=0\), where
\(D=\mathrm d/\mathrm dz\) and \(P\) is monic of degree \(p\ge2\) with
complex coefficients.

For evolution equations of even order (\(p\) odd), and for purely
dispersive equations (only odd-order derivatives), we give a complete
algebraic description of all nonconstant globally meromorphic
travelling waves: they are rational, rational in an exponential, or
elliptic, with one pole per period.

For \(p\ge3\) such waves are exceptional: a generic equation of the
family has none. We count the exceptions with the periods of the wave
fixed. Across the whole family there are \(\binom{2p-1}{p-1}\)
normalized one-pole pairs \((P,v)\) with \(v\) rational in an
exponential of given rate, and \(2\binom{2p-2}{p-2}\) with \(v\)
elliptic on a given generic lattice. We also count how many of these
belong to the purely dispersive subfamily (\(p\) even) and to the
purely dissipative one (only even-order derivatives in the linear
part, \(p\) odd). Each contains
\(F_p=\binom{p-1}{\lfloor(p-1)/2\rfloor}\) of the rational-exponential
pairs: all solitary pulses in the former, all fronts in the latter.
The purely dispersive subfamily also contains \(2F_p\) of the elliptic
pairs.

When \(2p-1\) is prime, these statements are proved and all pairs are
distinct. Exact computer certificates establish the counts, with
multiplicities, for \(2\le p\le73\). We conjecture the same in every
order.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}
Meromorphic travelling waves; dispersive and dissipative equations;
elliptic functions; polynomial convolution; algebraic multiplicity.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Exact travelling waves of nonlinear evolution equations are usually
found one equation at a time, and usually only for special values of
the coefficients. The generalized Kuramoto--Sivashinsky equation is a
classical example. It has nonconstant travelling waves
that are polynomials in a hyperbolic tangent at exactly four values of
its squared dimensionless dispersion parameter. Kuramoto and Tsuzuki
found the dispersionless one \citep{KuramotoTsuzuki1976}, Kudryashov the
other three \citep{Kudryashov1988,Kudryashov1990}, and Eremenko proved
that, together with Kudryashov's elliptic solution and a single rational
one, they exhaust the nonconstant meromorphic travelling waves
\citep{Eremenko2006}.
Counted as we count below, the tanh list has ten members. This paper
shows that ten is \(\binom52\): the classical list is the order-three
case of a binomial enumeration, proved for infinitely many orders and
conjectured for all.

We consider the evolution equations
\[
 u_t+uu_x+\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_j\partial_x^{j+1}u=0,\qquad b_p\ne0,
\]
which contain KdV--Burgers (\(p=2\)), Kuramoto--Sivashinsky (\(p=3\))
and Kawahara (\(p=4\)), the sixth-order turbulence models of
\citet{KudryashovMigita2007,SimbawaMatthewsCox2010}, and the quadratic
long-wave reductions of \citet{KudryashovSinelshchikovChernyavsky2008}.
A travelling wave \(u=w(x-ct)\) satisfies, after one integration,
\(\sum_jb_jw^{(j)}-cw+w^2/2+K=0\). Shifting \(w\) by a constant
solution and normalizing the leading coefficient gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 E_P(v):=P(D)v+\frac12v^2=0,\qquad
 P(\rho)=\rho^p+\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}a_j\rho^j,\qquad p\ge2,
\end{equation}
with \(D=\mathrm d/\mathrm dz\) and complex coefficients. The speed,
the integration constant and the background are absorbed into
\(a_0\) (Section~\ref{sec:setting}). Throughout, \(p\) is the order of
\eqref{eq:main}, one less than the order of the evolution equation.

Two subfamilies play a special role. In the evolution equation,
derivatives of odd order are dispersive terms and derivatives of even
order are dissipative terms. We call an equation \emph{purely
dispersive} if it contains only odd-order derivatives, as the KdV and
Kawahara equations do; then \(p\) is even and \(P\) is an even
polynomial. We call it \emph{purely dissipative} if its linear part
contains only even-order derivatives, as for the
Kuramoto--Sivashinsky equation without dispersion; then \(p\) is odd
and \(P-P(0)\) is an odd polynomial. All other equations, such as
KdV--Burgers, are \emph{mixed}. The two pure subfamilies are the
equations that are invariant under a reflection of \(x\), and for their
travelling waves this reflection is \(z\mapsto-z\).

\paragraph{Classification.}
Eremenko's theorem states that an autonomous algebraic differential
equation with a single monomial of top degree and finitely many Laurent
expansions at a pole has only three kinds of nonconstant meromorphic
solutions: rational, rational in an exponential, and elliptic
\citep{Eremenko2006}. Theorem~\ref{thm:complete} verifies these
hypotheses for \eqref{eq:main} in two cases. The first is \(p\) odd,
that is, every evolution equation of even order: there the Laurent
expansion at a pole is unique. The second is the purely dispersive
case: there one Laurent coefficient is free, but \eqref{eq:main} is
invariant under \(z\mapsto-z\) and conserves an energy, and fixing the
energy fixes that coefficient. In both cases every nonconstant
meromorphic travelling wave is of one of the three kinds and has one
pole per period. A fixed equation has at most one such wave up to
translation when \(p\) is odd, and at most one on each energy level
when it is purely dispersive. The mixed equations of odd order are not
covered, and cannot be: Section~\ref{sec:open} recalls a second-order
example with other meromorphic waves. Section~\ref{sec:systems} writes
each of the three kinds as a finite polynomial system, following the
principal-part construction of
\citet{DeminaKudryashov2011,DeminaKudryashovElliptic2011}.

\paragraph{The count.}
For \(p\ge3\) such waves are exceptional: a generic equation of the
family has none (Section~\ref{sec:fixed}). We count the exceptions,
with the periods of the wave fixed.
For waves that are rational in an exponential, fix the rate to one and
place the pole at \(i\pi\).
Every solution of \eqref{eq:main} that is rational in \(e^z\), has
one pole modulo \(2\pi i\ZZ\) and vanishes as \(e^z\to0\) is of the
form \(v=s_pA(D)Q\), where \(Q=1/(1+e^{-z})\), \(s_p\) is a fixed
scale and \(A\) is a monic polynomial of degree \(p-1\).
Let \(P\) range over all monic polynomials of degree \(p\) and count
the pairs \((P,v)\) for which \(v\) solves \eqref{eq:main}.
For elliptic waves, fix a nonsingular Weierstrass lattice and count the
pairs \((P,v)\) with \(v\) elliptic on that lattice and its only pole
at zero. The answers are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 N_p&=\binom{2p-1}{p-1}
     &&\text{rational-exponential pairs},\\
 M_p&=2\binom{2p-2}{p-2}
     &&\text{elliptic pairs on a generic lattice}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Whenever \(2p-1\) is prime, both formulas hold and every pair is
simple, that is, nondegenerate; in the elliptic case this is for a
generic lattice (Theorems~\ref{thm:prime} and~\ref{thm:elliptic-count}).
For every \(2\le p\le73\), the formulas hold as counts with
multiplicity (Section~\ref{sec:further}). On special lattices elliptic
pairs do merge, and Section~\ref{sec:ks} determines where this happens
at order three.
At \(p=5\), where \(2p-1=9\) is the first composite index, a separate
exact computation shows that all \(126\) rational-exponential pairs
are simple.
We conjecture the same counting and simplicity statements in every
order (Conjecture~\ref{conj:count}). The counts are over \(\CC\) and
range over all monic \(P\), mixed operators included. The
classification is used only to know that, where it applies, there are
no other meromorphic waves.

\paragraph{Purely dispersive and purely dissipative equations.}
The reflection \(z\mapsto-z\) acts on the pairs, and the pairs that it
fixes are exactly those whose equation is purely dispersive, for even
\(p\), or purely dissipative, for odd \(p\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:symmetric}). Under the same hypotheses as for
\(N_p\), their number is
\[
 F_p=\binom{p-1}{\lfloor(p-1)/2\rfloor},
\]
and the purely dispersive equations also account for \(2F_p\) of the
elliptic pairs on a generic lattice
(Corollary~\ref{cor:elliptic-symmetric}).
The two subfamilies carry different kinds of waves. A purely
dispersive equation has only pulses, which return to the same constant
at both ends. A purely dissipative equation has only fronts, which
connect two different constants; this is proved whenever all pairs are
distinct, and for real waves in every order. For example, the Kawahara
counts \(3\) and \(6\) are \(F_4\) and \(2F_4\); the seventh-order KdV
family has ten pairs, two of them real; and the six pairs of the purely
dissipative family of order five contain the four kink solutions of
\citet{KudryashovMigita2007} (Section~\ref{sec:examples}).

\paragraph{A single equation.}
The count is over operators, and Section~\ref{sec:fixed} translates it
back to one evolution equation. Rescaling \(x\) moves the coefficient
vector of an equation along a weighted curve, and this is why the rate
is fixed in the count. An equation has a one-pole travelling wave that
is rational in an exponential if and only if its coefficient vector
lies on one of at most \((N_p+F_p)/2\) such curves, and then it has
finitely many (Corollary~\ref{cor:solvable}). For odd
\(5\le p\le73\), the equations with any nonconstant meromorphic
travelling wave form a set of dimension at most two in the
\((p-1)\)-dimensional space of coefficients, so a generic equation has
none (Corollary~\ref{cor:generic}). At \(p=3\) this set is the union of
the four classical Kuramoto--Sivashinsky curves.

\paragraph{Method.}
For rational-exponential profiles, the pair \((P,v_A)\) solves
\eqref{eq:main} if and only if \(A\) divides its own discrete
self-convolution \(S_A(n)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}A(j)A(n-j)\), and then
\(P=s_pS_A/(2A)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:divisibility}). When
\(\ell=2p-1\) is prime, \(\ell S_A\equiv\pm(\rho^\ell-\rho)\pmod\ell\),
so modulo \(\ell\) the condition becomes \(A\mid\rho^\ell-\rho\), and
its solutions are indexed by the \((p-1)\)-element subsets \(S\) of
\(\FF_\ell\). Each of them lifts by Hensel's lemma to a simple
solution, and a weighted B\'ezout bound shows that the lifts are all
the solutions. The reflection acts on the subsets by \(S\mapsto-S\),
which gives \(F_p\). For the elliptic pairs, the same B\'ezout argument
gives the count with multiplicity on every nonsingular lattice, once a
degeneration to the cusp excludes solutions at infinity. A degeneration
to the node, where the elliptic problem becomes the pulse case of the
exponential one with two choices of background, shows that the pairs
are simple on a generic lattice.

Two ingredients resist a uniform proof. One is a rigidity statement
for the continuous convolution \(\int_0^\rho A(t)A(\rho-t)\dd t\),
needed to exclude solutions at infinity; it is proved for prime and
prime-power indices, certified by exact arithmetic through degree
\(72\), and would give all counts with multiplicity in every order.
The other is simplicity of every rational-exponential pair, proved
for prime indices and, by exact computation, at \(p=5\), and open in
general (Conjecture~\ref{conj:count}).

\paragraph{Relation to earlier work.}
The classification theorem is a uniform version of results that exist
in pieces. For odd \(p\) the indicial polynomial has no positive integer
zero, the Laurent expansion at a pole is unique, and Eremenko's theorem
applies directly; this case is contained in
\citet[Theorem~1]{YuanLiQi2014}, with related odd-order results in
\citet{HuangZhangChenYuan2014,YuanWuChenHuang2014} and a fifth-order
instance in \citet{Dang2023Odd}. For even \(p\) the index \(3p\) is
resonant, and the conclusion need not hold for mixed operators: the
Fisher equation has an elliptic-exponential family of solutions
\citep{AblowitzZeppetella1979,Kudryashov2010,ConteNgWu2015} whose
generic members are meromorphic and of none of the three kinds.
The familiar Ablowitz--Zeppetella front is a rational-exponential
degeneration of this family.
When \(P\) is an even polynomial the equation has a first integral,
the energy, and fixing its value removes the resonant freedom.
This is the device of
\citet[Lemmas~3--4]{EremenkoLiaoNg2009} for \(P=D^p+a_0\), stated as a
general step by \citet{ConteNgWu2018,ConteMusetteNgWu2025} and applied
to the Kawahara equation \citep{DeminaKudryashovKawahara2010,YeEtAl2022},
the generalized Bretherton equation
\citep{DeminaKudryashov2011,KudryashovSinelshchikovDemina2011} and the
seventh-order KdV equation \citep{Dang2023Even}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:complete} extends it uniformly to every even \(P\),
intermediate derivatives included. Equations with two dominant
monomials, or without the finiteness property, are treated by other
methods in \citet{Demina2019,Demina2022}.
The general enumeration has no precedent that we know of; its special
cases at \(p=3\) and in the Kawahara family are classical, as
the examples make explicit.

\paragraph{Organization.}
Section~\ref{sec:setting} fixes the normalizations and the vocabulary.
Sections~\ref{sec:completeness} and~\ref{sec:systems} contain the
classification and the three polynomial systems.
Sections~\ref{sec:counting} and~\ref{sec:elliptic-counting} prove the
two counts, Section~\ref{sec:further} records which orders are
settled, and Section~\ref{sec:fixed} reads the count for a single
equation. Section~\ref{sec:examples} works out the lowest orders and
the two pure subfamilies, including the lattices on which elliptic
pairs merge. Section~\ref{sec:open} collects the open questions, and
Appendix~\ref{app:verification} describes the exact verification and
the ancillary material. All counts are over \(\CC\); the examples
identify the real profiles, and stability is not discussed.
