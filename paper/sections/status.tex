\section{Other orders and open questions}\label{sec:further}

At a prime index \(\ell=2p-1\), reduction modulo \(\ell\) gives
rigidity and the simplicity of every pair. At the other orders these are
two hypotheses, and the weighted B\'ezout bound replaces the labels
(companion note \texttt{anc/general\_orders.pdf} in the source package).
The note proves the next proposition, Proposition~\ref{prop:bezout},
rigidity for prime powers \(2p-1\) and the general reduction lemma
behind Step~3 of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:prime}, and it gives an
algebraic proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:conservation}(i)--(ii).

\begin{proposition}[Every order]\label{prop:general}
Let \(p\ge2\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] The isolated solutions of the matching equations,
counted with multiplicity, number at most \(N_p\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bezout}), and those with \(\iota A=A\), as
solutions of the matching equations of the pure subfamily, at most
\(F_p\) (Remark~\ref{rem:Fp-bound}).
\item[\textup{(ii)}] If rigidity \((\mathrm R_{p-1})\) holds, all the
systems below have finitely many solutions, and counted with
multiplicity there are exactly \(N_p\) rational-exponential pairs
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bezout}), \(S_p\) solutions of the pulse
equations \eqref{eq:pulse-equations}, \(F_p\) solutions of the matching
equations of the pure subfamily, \(M_p\) elliptic pairs on every
lattice and, for even \(p\), \(2F_p\) elliptic pairs with an even
operator.
\item[\textup{(iii)}] If, in addition, every rational-exponential pair
is simple, then all the conclusions of Theorems~\ref{thm:prime}(a),
\ref{thm:elliptic-count} and~\ref{thm:symmetric},
Proposition~\ref{cor:endpoints} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:elliptic-symmetric} hold at this \(p\), except for
the labelling.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\emph{Rigidity} \((\mathrm R_{p-1})\) is proved when \(2p-1\) is a prime
or a prime power. Exact integer certificates in the source package
establish it for \(p\le73\) (degree \(d=p-1\le72\)), and finite-field
Gr\"obner computations in the repository
(\texttt{certificates/rigidity\_572/}), checked by independent
pipelines, for \(p\le573\). So the counts with multiplicity of
part~(ii) hold for \(2\le p\le573\).

\emph{Simplicity} of all pairs is established by exact computations at
\(p=5\), \(8\) and \(11\) (Appendix~\ref{app:verification}). The other
orders up to twelve have a prime index, so part~(iii) applies for every
\(p\le12\); the first order at which distinctness is not known is
\(p=13\). Inside the two pure subfamilies a much smaller computation
shows that the \(F_p\) pairs are distinct for every \(p\le14\).

\begin{corollary}[Orders up to twelve]\label{cor:twelve}
For every \(2\le p\le12\) there are exactly \(N_p\) rational-exponential
pairs, all simple, of which \(S_p\) are pulses and \(F_p\) belong to
the pure subfamily of that order; and a generic lattice carries exactly
\(M_p\) distinct elliptic pairs. In particular this holds for all the
equations of Table~\ref{tab:named}.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Open questions.}\label{sec:open}
The first is rigidity \eqref{eq:rigidity} in every degree;
Lemma~\ref{lem:laplace} says what it means for rational waves. The
second is whether every point of \(\Xp\) is simple, that is, whether the
tangent map of the matching equations at a solution \(S_A=AC_A\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent}
 J_A(h)=\remA\bigl(2(A\star h)-C_Ah\bigr),\qquad \deg h<d=p-1,
\end{equation}
always has zero kernel. At a prime index this holds already modulo
\(\ell\), where the map is multiplication by a polynomial coprime to
\(\overline A\) \eqref{eq:prime-jacobian}; a uniform replacement is
missing at composite indices. Simplicity does not follow from the
uniqueness of the Laurent expansion in Theorem~\ref{thm:complete}: that
concerns a fixed operator, whereas in \eqref{eq:tangent} the operator
varies with the profile.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:count}
Rigidity \eqref{eq:rigidity} holds in every degree, and the matching
Jacobian \eqref{eq:tangent} is invertible at every point of \(\Xp\).
Consequently there are exactly \(\binom{2p-1}{p-1}\)
rational-exponential pairs, all distinct, for every \(p\ge2\).
\end{conjecture}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:general}(iii) the conjecture would extend
Corollary~\ref{cor:twelve} to every order, and the rational-exponential
waves of purely dissipative equations would all be fronts. Simplicity on
every lattice is false already at orders two \eqref{eq:kdvb-elliptic}
and three \eqref{eq:ks-collisions}. For mixed equations of even order
\(p\ge4\) a separate question is open (Remark~\ref{rem:fisher}).
