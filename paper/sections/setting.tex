\section{Equations, waves and what is counted}\label{sec:setting}

This section fixes the normalizations and the vocabulary used in the
rest of the paper. It contains no new result.

\subsection{From the evolution equation to the profile equation}

Let \(u=w(\xi)\), \(\xi=x-ct\), be a travelling wave of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pde}
 u_t+uu_x+\sum_{j=1}^{p}b_j\partial_x^{j+1}u=0,\qquad b_p\ne0 .
\end{equation}
One integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integrated}
 \sum_{j=1}^{p}b_jw^{(j)}-cw+\frac12w^2+K=0
\end{equation}
with a constant \(K\). Let \(w_\ast\) be a constant solution of
\eqref{eq:integrated}, that is, a root of \(w^2/2-cw+K\), and let
\(\lambda\ne0\). The substitution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
 w(\xi)=w_\ast+b_p\lambda^p\,v(\lambda\xi)
\end{equation}
turns \eqref{eq:integrated} into the profile equation \eqref{eq:main}
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficients}
 a_j=\frac{b_j}{b_p}\lambda^{j-p}\quad(1\le j\le p-1),\qquad
 a_0=\frac{w_\ast-c}{b_p\lambda^p}.
\end{equation}
Thus \(a_1,\ldots,a_{p-1}\) record the equation, up to the scale
\(\lambda\), and \(a_0\) records the speed of the wave relative to the
background \(w_\ast\). The scale is free at this stage. It will be
fixed by the period of the wave.

The constant solutions of \eqref{eq:main} are \(v=0\) and \(v=-2a_0\);
they are the two roots \(w_\ast\). Choosing the other root replaces the
pair \((P,v)\) by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
 \bigl(P-2P(0),\ v+2P(0)\bigr),
\end{equation}
which again satisfies \eqref{eq:main}, as a direct substitution shows.
The two pairs describe the same wave of the same evolution equation.

\subsection{Dispersive, dissipative and mixed equations}

In \eqref{eq:pde} a derivative of odd order is a dispersive term, and a
derivative of even order is a dissipative term: for real coefficients
the first changes the phase of a Fourier mode and the second its
amplitude. We use the following names.
\begin{itemize}
\item The equation is \emph{purely dispersive} if it contains only
odd-order derivatives, that is, \(b_j=0\) for odd \(j\). Then \(p\) is
even, \(P\) is an even polynomial, and the equation is invariant under
\((x,t)\mapsto(-x,-t)\). Examples are the KdV equation (\(p=2\)), the
Kawahara equation (\(p=4\)) and the seventh-order KdV equation
(\(p=6\)).
\item The equation is \emph{purely dissipative} if its linear part
contains only even-order derivatives, that is, \(b_j=0\) for even
\(j\). Then \(p\) is odd, \(P-P(0)\) is an odd polynomial, and the
equation is invariant under \((x,u)\mapsto(-x,-u)\). Examples are the
Kuramoto--Sivashinsky equation without dispersion (\(p=3\)) and the
Nikolaevskiy equation (\(p=5\)); see \citet{SimbawaMatthewsCox2010}.
\item All other equations are \emph{mixed}. Examples are the
KdV--Burgers equation and the Kuramoto--Sivashinsky equation with
dispersion.
\end{itemize}
The two pure subfamilies are described by one condition. We call a
monic polynomial \(P\) of degree \(p\) \emph{reflection-symmetric} if
\(P-P(0)\) has the parity of \(\rho^p\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-class}
 P(-\rho)=P(\rho)\quad(p\text{ even}),\qquad
 P(\rho)+P(-\rho)=2P(0)\quad(p\text{ odd}).
\end{equation}
In the travelling-wave variable both symmetries are the reflection
\(z\mapsto-z\). If \(P\) is even and \(v(z)\) solves \eqref{eq:main},
so does \(v(-z)\). If \(p\) is odd and \(P-P(0)\) is odd, so does
\(-v(-z)-2P(0)\); this is the reflection combined with the change of
background \eqref{eq:shift}.

\subsection{Waves, poles, pulses and fronts}

Throughout, a meromorphic function is single-valued and meromorphic on
the whole complex plane. Three kinds of meromorphic travelling waves
occur in this paper: rational in \(z\), rational in an exponential
\(e^{\kappa z}\), and elliptic. For the equations covered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:complete} there are no others; for the remaining
equations these are the waves that we count. The period group of such a
wave is its \emph{full}
group of periods: trivial in the first case, infinite cyclic in the
second, a lattice in the third. We say that the wave has \emph{one pole
per period} if its poles form a single coset of this group. There is
then one pole in the rational case, one pole modulo the primitive
period in the second case, and one pole in each period parallelogram in
the elliptic case.

A wave that is rational in \(e^{\kappa z}\) and has one pole per period
has finite limits as \(e^{\kappa z}\to0\) and \(\infty\)
(Section~\ref{sec:systems}). Both limits are constant solutions. The
wave is a \emph{pulse} if the two limits coincide and a \emph{front}
otherwise. For a real rate and a real profile these are the solitary
waves and the kinks of the evolution equation, and they are smooth and
bounded on the real axis when the pole lies off it. In
\eqref{eq:scaling} we take \(w_\ast\) to be the limit as
\(e^{\kappa z}\to0\). Then \(v\to0\) at that end, and the other limit
is \(0\) for a pulse and \(-2a_0\) for a front.

\subsection{What is counted}

We count pairs \((P,v)\): a monic polynomial \(P\) of degree \(p\) and
a solution \(v\) of \eqref{eq:main} of a given kind, with its periods
fixed.
\begin{itemize}
\item A \emph{rational-exponential pair} has \(v\) rational in
\(e^z\), with one pole modulo \(2\pi i\ZZ\), placed at \(i\pi\), and
\(v\to0\) as \(e^z\to0\).
\item An \emph{elliptic pair} on a lattice \(\Lambda\) has \(v\)
elliptic with period lattice \(\Lambda\), including its scale, and its
only pole modulo \(\Lambda\) at zero.
\end{itemize}
Fixing the period or the period lattice removes the scale \(\lambda\)
in \eqref{eq:scaling}, and placing the pole removes translations.
The operator \(P\) varies over \emph{all} monic polynomials of degree
\(p\), and all counts are over \(\CC\). A wave and its mirror image are
counted as two pairs when they are distinct; the pairs that coincide
with their mirror image are the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:reflection}. In the elliptic case both pairs
\eqref{eq:shift} are counted; in the rational-exponential case the
condition \(v\to0\) selects one of them. Section~\ref{sec:fixed}
translates the counts back to a single evolution equation.
