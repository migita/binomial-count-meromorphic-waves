\section{Mirror images and the two pure subfamilies}\label{sec:reflection}

This section counts the pairs of the two pure subfamilies. They are the
pairs that coincide with their mirror image \(\iota A\), where for monic
\(A\) of degree \(d=p-1\)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iota}
 (\iota A)(\rho)=(-1)^dA(-\rho),\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad
 \alpha_j\longmapsto(-1)^j\alpha_j .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Reflection]\label{prop:reflection}
For every monic \(A\) of degree \(d\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflection-S}
 S_{\iota A}(\rho)=-S_A(-\rho)-2A(0)A(-\rho),\qquad
 R_{\iota A}(\rho)=-R_A(-\rho).
\end{equation}
Hence \(\iota\) maps solutions of the matching equations to solutions,
and simple solutions to simple ones. At a point of \(\Xp\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflection-pair}
 v_{\iota A}(z)=(-1)^p\bigl(v_A(-z)-s_pA(0)\bigr),\qquad
 P_{\iota A}(\rho)=(-1)^p\bigl(P_A(-\rho)+s_pA(0)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

By \eqref{eq:reflection-pair}, \(\iota A\) is indeed the mirror image of
\(A\): the wave \(v_A\) read in the opposite direction, with the zero
limit moved back to the end \(e^z\to0\) and, for odd \(p\), the sign
changed. For \(p=2\), \(\iota\) exchanges the fronts \(12Q(2-Q)\) and
\(-12Q^2\) of \eqref{eq:kdvb-pairs}: \(12(1-Q)(1+Q)-12=-12Q^2\).

\begin{proof}
Since \(Q(-z)=1-Q(z)\) and \(D\) changes sign under \(z\mapsto-z\), we
have \(\bigl(A(D)Q\bigr)(-z)=A(0)-A(-D)Q=A(0)-(-1)^d(\iota A)(D)Q\),
which is the first formula in \eqref{eq:reflection-pair}. Evaluating
\eqref{eq:discrete-square} at \(-z\), and using it for \(\iota A\) on
the left, gives
\[
 A(0)^2-2A(0)A(-D)Q-S_{\iota A}(D)Q=-S_A(0)+S_A(-D)Q .
\]
The constant terms cancel because \(S_A(0)=-A(0)^2\)
(Section~\ref{sec:labels}), and the injectivity of \(F\mapsto F(D)Q\)
gives the first identity in \eqref{eq:reflection-S}. Substituting
\(S_A=AC_A+R_A\) at \(-\rho\), with \(A(-\rho)=(-1)^d(\iota A)(\rho)\),
turns it into a division of \(S_{\iota A}\) by the monic polynomial
\(\iota A\), which is unique. So \(R_{\iota A}(\rho)=-R_A(-\rho)\) and
\(C_{\iota A}(\rho)=(-1)^p\bigl(C_A(-\rho)+2A(0)\bigr)\). The first
relation reads \(R_r(\iota\alpha)=(-1)^{r+1}R_r(\alpha)\): the
invertible linear map \eqref{eq:iota} takes each matching equation to
plus or minus a matching equation, hence the assertion about solutions.
The second relation and \eqref{eq:operator-A} give \(P_{\iota A}\).
\end{proof}

A pair is fixed by \(\iota\) when \(\alpha_j=0\) for every odd \(j\); we
write \(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\) for the set of these pairs. On this
subspace \(\iota A=A\), so \(R_A\) is an odd polynomial by
\eqref{eq:reflection-S}, and the equations \(R_r=0\) with \(r\) even
hold identically. The \emph{matching equations of the subfamily} are the
equations \(R_r=0\), \(r\) odd, in the unknowns \(\alpha_j\), \(j\)
even; their solutions are the pairs of \(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\). Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fp}
 F_p=\binom{p-1}{\lfloor(p-1)/2\rfloor}=1,2,3,6,10,20\quad(p=2,\ldots,7).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Count in the two pure subfamilies]\label{thm:symmetric}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] For every \(p\), a pair \(A\) coincides with its
mirror image, \(\iota A=A\), if and only if \(P_A\) is
reflection-symmetric. Thus these pairs are exactly the
rational-exponential pairs of the purely dispersive equations when \(p\)
is even, and of the purely dissipative equations when \(p\) is odd.
\item[\textup{(ii)}] Let \(\ell=2p-1\) be prime. If \(A\) has label
\(L\), then \(\iota A\) has label \(-L\). Hence \(\iota A=A\) if and
only if \(L=-L\), and there are exactly \(F_p\) such pairs. Each of them
is a simple solution of the matching equations of its subfamily.
\item[\textup{(iii)}] The rational-exponential pairs of a purely
dispersive equation are pulses, for every even \(p\). Those of a purely
dissipative equation are fronts: when \(2p-1\) is prime, and for pairs
with real coefficients at every odd \(p\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

In Table~\ref{tab:ks} the two pairs with \(a=0\) are the \(F_3=2\) pairs
of the Kuramoto--Sivashinsky equation without dispersion; their labels
\(\{1,4\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\) satisfy \(L=-L\). Both are fronts; the
pulses \((\pm1,0)\) need dispersion.

\begin{remark}[Every order]\label{rem:Fp-bound}
At every order the isolated solutions of the matching equations of the
subfamily, counted with multiplicity, number at most \(F_p\), the
B\'ezout number of this system (companion note, Theorem~4.1).
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:symmetric}]
\emph{(i).} If \(\iota A=A\), then \eqref{eq:reflection-pair} gives
\(P_A(\rho)=(-1)^p(P_A(-\rho)+s_pA(0))\). For even \(p\), \(A\) is odd,
so \(A(0)=0\) and \(P_A\) is even. For odd \(p\),
\(P_A(\rho)+P_A(-\rho)=-s_pA(0)\): \(P_A\) is reflection-symmetric, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-endpoint}
 s_pA(0)=-2P_A(0)\qquad(p\text{ odd}).
\end{equation}

Conversely, let \(P=P_A\) be reflection-symmetric. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:symmetric-waves} at the pole \(i\pi\), and by the
period \(2\pi i\), we have \(v_A(-z)=v_A(z)\) if \(p\) is even and
\(-v_A(-z)=v_A(z)+2P(0)\) if \(p\) is odd. So \eqref{eq:reflection-pair}
becomes \(v_{\iota A}=v_A+c\) with a constant \(c\), and \(c=0\) because
both profiles tend to zero as \(z\to-\infty\). Hence \(\iota A=A\) by
the uniqueness of the form \eqref{eq:normalized-A}.

\emph{(ii).} The map \eqref{eq:iota} negates the roots of
\(\overline A\), so the label of \(\iota A\) is \(-L\). A label
determines its pair (Theorem~\ref{thm:prime}), so \(\iota A=A\) exactly
when \(L=-L\). The nonzero residues modulo \(\ell=2d+1\) form \(d\)
pairs \(\{s,-s\}\), and a set \(L=-L\) of \(d\) elements consists of
\(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\) of these pairs, together with \(0\) if \(d\) is
odd. So there are \(\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}=F_p\) such sets.

Differentiating \(R_r(\iota\alpha)=(-1)^{r+1}R_r(\alpha)\) at a fixed
point shows that \(\partial R_r/\partial\alpha_j=0\) unless
\(j\equiv r+1\) modulo two. So the Jacobian matrix of the full system
splits into two square blocks. It is invertible, so both blocks are, and
one of them is the Jacobian of the equations of the subfamily.

\emph{(iii).} For even \(p\), \(A(0)=0\) as in (i). Let \(p\) be odd. If
\(2p-1\) is prime, a label with \(L=-L\) has an even number \(d\) of
elements, so it does not contain \(0\), and the pair is a front by
Proposition~\ref{cor:endpoints}. For general odd \(p\), let a point of
\(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\) with real coefficients be a pulse, \(A(0)=0\).
Then \(P_A(0)=0\) by \eqref{eq:symmetric-endpoint}. Now \(v_A\) is real
on the real axis and tends to zero with all its derivatives at both
ends, so integrating \eqref{eq:main} along the real axis, with
\(P_A(0)=0\), gives \(\int v_A^2\dd z=0\), which is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Elliptic pairs of purely dispersive equations]
\label{cor:elliptic-symmetric}
Let \(p\) be even, with \(2p-1\) prime. On every lattice, the elliptic
pairs whose operator is an even polynomial are those with \(u_j=0\) for
odd \(j\) in \eqref{eq:elliptic-enumeration-profile}, and counted with
multiplicity there are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-symmetric-length}
 2F_p
\end{equation}
of them. On a generic lattice they are distinct.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:symmetric-waves} an even operator has an even
profile, and \eqref{eq:elliptic-enumeration-profile} is even exactly
when \(u_j=0\) for odd \(j\). Conversely, for an even profile the odd
part of \eqref{eq:main} is \(\sum_{j\text{ odd}}a_jD^jV=0\); the
\(D^jV\) have distinct pole orders, so \(a_j=0\) for odd \(j\). We
follow the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptic-count} on the subspace
\(u_j=0\), \(j\) odd. There the residual of Step~1 is even, so only the
unknowns and the equations of even weight in \eqref{eq:elliptic-weights}
remain, \(p/2\) of each. The omitted leading forms vanish identically on
the subspace, so by Step~3 the others have only the trivial common zero,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:conservation} applies to the restricted system;
multiplicities refer to it. For the nodal parameters
\eqref{eq:nodal-parameters} reflection is \(Q\mapsto1-Q\), and an even
profile is \(\beta+v_A\) with \(\iota A=A\). The computation of Step~5
on this subspace gives \eqref{eq:nodal-matching} with
\(A\in\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\): the matching equations of the subfamily, and
the quadratic for \(\beta\). By Theorem~\ref{thm:symmetric} there are
\(F_p\) such \(A\), all pulses, each a simple solution of these
equations, and \(P_A(0)\ne0\) by Proposition~\ref{cor:endpoints}. So the
restricted nodal system has \(2F_p\) solutions, all simple, and the rest
of Step~5 applies without change.
\end{proof}

For odd \(p\) there is no such count. The profile of a purely
dissipative equation is \(-P(0)\) plus a function odd about the pole
(Corollary~\ref{cor:symmetric-waves}), and its matching system has one
equation more than unknowns. For \(p=3,5,7\) an exact computation
(\texttt{elliptic\_dissipative.py}, Appendix~\ref{app:verification})
finds no solution on a sample lattice, hence there is none on a generic
lattice (the entries \(0\) of Table~\ref{tab:named}): rigidity holds at
these orders (Section~\ref{sec:further}), so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:conservation} and the elimination in the proof of its
part~(iii) the parameters with such pairs form an algebraic subset of
the \((g_2,g_3)\)-plane, invariant under rescaling as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:elliptic-count}(b). At \(p=3\) such pairs occur exactly
when \(g_2=0\): a purely dissipative operator has \(B=0\) in
\eqref{eq:ks-elliptic-scheme}, and then \(P=D^3+a_0\) and \(g_2=0\). At
\(p=5\) elliptic waves of the Nikolaevskiy equation on special lattices
are given by \citet{Kudryashov2004} and \citet{KudryashovDemina2007}.
