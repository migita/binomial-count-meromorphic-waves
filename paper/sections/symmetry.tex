\subsection{Mirror images: the two pure subfamilies}\label{sec:reflection}

The numbers \(N_p\) and \(M_p\) refer to the full monic family. We now
count the pairs whose equation is purely dispersive or purely
dissipative. These are the pairs that coincide with their own mirror
image under the reflection \(z\mapsto-z\). On the labels the reflection
is \(S\mapsto-S\), and the number of labels with \(S=-S\) is again a
binomial coefficient.

For a monic polynomial \(A\) of degree \(d=p-1\) put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iota}
 (\iota A)(\rho)=(-1)^dA(-\rho),\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad
 \alpha_j\longmapsto(-1)^j\alpha_j .
\end{equation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:reflection} shows that \(\iota\) is the reflection
\(z\mapsto-z\) written in the coordinates \(\alpha_j\). It reads the
wave \(v_A\) in the opposite direction and then restores the
normalization: it moves the zero limit back to the end \(e^z\to0\), and
for odd \(p\) it changes the sign of the profile.


\begin{proposition}[Reflection]\label{prop:reflection}
For every monic \(A\) of degree \(d\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflection-S}
 S_{\iota A}(\rho)=-S_A(-\rho)-2A(0)A(-\rho),\qquad
 R_{\iota A}(\rho)=-R_A(-\rho).
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\iota\) maps solutions of the matching equations to
solutions, and simple solutions to simple solutions.
At a point of \(\Xp\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflection-pair}
 v_{\iota A}(z)=(-1)^p\bigl(v_A(-z)-s_pA(0)\bigr),\qquad
 P_{\iota A}(\rho)=(-1)^p\bigl(P_A(-\rho)+s_pA(0)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The pairs \(A\) and \(\iota A\) are the mirror images that \(N_p\)
counts separately. For \(p=3\) the involution is
\((a,b)\mapsto(-a,b)\) in Table~\ref{tab:ks}.

\begin{proof}
Since \(Q(-z)=1-Q(z)\), and \(D\) changes sign under \(z\mapsto-z\),
\[
 \bigl(A(D)Q\bigr)(-z)=A(0)-A(-D)Q=A(0)-(-1)^d(\iota A)(D)Q .
\]
This is the first formula in \eqref{eq:reflection-pair}.
Evaluate \eqref{eq:discrete-square} at \(-z\), and use
\eqref{eq:discrete-square} for \(\iota A\) on the left:
\[
 A(0)^2-2A(0)A(-D)Q-S_{\iota A}(D)Q=-S_A(0)+S_A(-D)Q .
\]
Every \(B(D)Q\) is a polynomial in \(Q\) without constant term, so
letting \(z\to-\infty\) gives \(S_A(0)=-A(0)^2\). The remaining terms,
and the injectivity of \(B\mapsto B(D)Q\), give the first identity in
\eqref{eq:reflection-S}. Now substitute \(S_A=AC_A+R_A\) at \(-\rho\):
\[
 S_{\iota A}(\rho)
 =(\iota A)(\rho)\,(-1)^p\bigl(C_A(-\rho)+2A(0)\bigr)-R_A(-\rho).
\]
Division by the monic polynomial \(\iota A\) is unique. Hence
\[
 R_{\iota A}(\rho)=-R_A(-\rho),\qquad
 C_{\iota A}(\rho)=(-1)^p\bigl(C_A(-\rho)+2A(0)\bigr).
\]
The first relation reads \(R_r(\iota\alpha)=(-1)^{r+1}R_r(\alpha)\), so
the linear map \eqref{eq:iota} preserves the ideal of the matching
equations. The second relation and \eqref{eq:operator-A} give the
formula for \(P_{\iota A}\).
\end{proof}

A pair is fixed by \(\iota\) when \(\alpha_j=0\) for every odd \(j\).
We write \(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\) for the set of these pairs, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fp}
 F_p=\binom{p-1}{\lfloor (p-1)/2\rfloor}.
\end{equation}
Its first values are \(1,2,3,6,10,20\) for \(p=2,\ldots,7\);
Table~\ref{tab:named} lists the equations to which they belong.

\begin{theorem}[Count in the two pure subfamilies]\label{thm:symmetric}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] For every \(p\), a pair \(A\) coincides with its
mirror image, \(\iota A=A\), if and only if \(P_A\) is
reflection-symmetric. Thus \(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\) consists of the
rational-exponential pairs of the purely dispersive equations when
\(p\) is even, and of the purely dissipative equations when \(p\) is
odd.
\item[\textup{(ii)}] Let \(\ell=2p-1\) be prime. If \(A\) has label
\(S\), then \(\iota A\) has label \(-S\). Hence \(\iota A=A\) if and
only if \(S=-S\), and there are exactly \(F_p\) such pairs. Each of
them is a simple solution of the matching equations of its subfamily.
\item[\textup{(iii)}] For even \(p\), every pair of
\(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\) is a pulse. For odd \(p\) with \(2p-1\) prime,
every pair of \(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\) is a front; for every odd \(p\),
every pair of \(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\) with real coefficients is a front.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

In plain terms, the pairs that coincide with their mirror image are
exactly the pairs of the purely dispersive equations, for even \(p\),
or of the purely dissipative equations, for odd \(p\). When \(2p-1\) is
prime there are exactly \(F_p\) of them, and they are the pairs whose
label is symmetric. The rational-exponential waves of a purely
dispersive equation are pulses, for every even \(p\). Those of a
purely dissipative equation are fronts: when \(2p-1\) is prime, and
for waves with real coefficients at every odd \(p\). Elliptic waves
occur in both subfamilies; the distinction between pulses and fronts
does not apply to them. Section~\ref{sec:further} records what is known
at the remaining orders.

\begin{proof}
\emph{(i).} If \(\iota A=A\), then \eqref{eq:reflection-pair} gives
\(P_A(\rho)=(-1)^p(P_A(-\rho)+s_pA(0))\). For even \(p\) the
polynomial \(A\) is odd, so \(A(0)=0\) and \(P_A\) is even. For odd
\(p\) the sum \(P_A(\rho)+P_A(-\rho)\) is the constant \(-s_pA(0)\).
In both cases \(P_A\) is reflection-symmetric, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-endpoint}
 s_pA(0)=-2P_A(0)\qquad(p\text{ odd}).
\end{equation}

Conversely, let \(P=P_A\) be reflection-symmetric, and apply
Corollary~\ref{cor:symmetric-waves} at the pole \(i\pi\), using the
period \(2\pi i\). If \(p\) is even, then \(v_A(-z)=v_A(z)\). Letting
\(z\to-\infty\) gives \(A(0)=0\), and \eqref{eq:reflection-pair}
becomes \(v_{\iota A}=v_A\). If \(p\) is odd, then
\(-v_A(-z)=v_A(z)+2P(0)\), and \eqref{eq:reflection-pair} becomes
\(v_{\iota A}(z)=v_A(z)+2P(0)+s_pA(0)\). Letting \(z\to-\infty\) shows
that the constant vanishes. In both cases \(v_{\iota A}=v_A\), hence
\(\iota A=A\) by the uniqueness of the form \eqref{eq:normalized-A}.

\emph{(ii).} The map \eqref{eq:iota} negates the roots of
\(\overline A\), so the label of \(\iota A\) is \(-S\). A label
determines its pair (Theorem~\ref{thm:prime}), so \(\iota A=A\) exactly
when \(S=-S\). To count these labels, note that the nonzero residues
modulo \(\ell=2d+1\) form \(d\) pairs \(\{s,-s\}\). A set \(S=-S\) of
\(d\) elements consists of \(d/2\) such pairs if \(d\) is even, and of
\(0\) and \((d-1)/2\) pairs if \(d\) is odd. In both cases there are
\(\binom{d}{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}=F_p\) sets.

On the subspace \(\{\alpha_j=0,\ j\text{ odd}\}\) we have
\(\iota A=A\), and \eqref{eq:reflection-S} shows that \(R_A\) is an odd
polynomial. The equations \(R_r=0\) with \(r\) even therefore hold
identically there, and the matching equations of the subfamily are the
equations \(R_r=0\), \(r\) odd, in the unknowns \(\alpha_j\), \(j\)
even. Differentiating the relation
\(R_r(\iota\alpha)=(-1)^{r+1}R_r(\alpha)\) at a fixed point shows that
\(\partial R_r/\partial\alpha_j=0\) unless \(j\equiv r+1\) modulo two.
The Jacobian matrix of the full system therefore splits into two square
blocks. It is invertible, so both blocks are, and one of them is the
Jacobian of the equations of the subfamily.

\emph{(iii).} For even \(p\) the polynomial \(A\) is odd, so \(A(0)=0\).
Let \(p\) be odd. If \(2p-1\) is prime, a label with \(S=-S\) has an
even number \(d\) of elements, so it does not contain \(0\), and the
pair is a front by Proposition~\ref{cor:endpoints}. For a general odd
\(p\), suppose that a point of \(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\) with real
coefficients is a pulse, \(A(0)=0\). Then \(P_A(0)=0\) by
\eqref{eq:symmetric-endpoint}. Now \(v_A\) is real on the real axis,
and it tends to zero with all its derivatives at both ends. Integrating
\eqref{eq:main} along the real axis, and using \(P_A(0)=0\), gives
\(\int v_A^2\dd z=0\). Hence \(v_A=0\), which is impossible.
\end{proof}
