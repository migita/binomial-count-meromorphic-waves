\section{Exact verification and ancillary material}\label{app:verification}

This appendix records one lemma used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:prime} and describes the computer checks that
accompany the paper.

The lemma transfers the absence of solutions at infinity from
characteristic \(\ell\) to characteristic zero.
Let homogeneous polynomials \(F_1,\ldots,F_m\), with coefficients
in \(\ZZ_{(\ell)}\), have no nonzero common zero over
\(\overline{\FF}_\ell\).
Then they have no nonzero common zero over \(\CC\).
Indeed a complex projective zero yields a zero over
\(\overline{\QQ}\) after fixing one nonzero coordinate.
Choose a place above \(\ell\), extend it if necessary, and scale
all coordinates by the inverse of a coordinate of smallest valuation.
The coordinates are integral and one is a unit.
Their reductions give a nonzero common zero over
\(\overline{\FF}_\ell\), contradicting the hypothesis.
The argument needs the algebraic closure of the residue field;
a search only over \(\FF_\ell\) would not suffice.

The ancillary material contains four kinds of checks.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{verify\_manuscript.py} reconstructs the discrete and
continuous convolutions, the low-order Laurent coefficients, and the
Weierstrass differential algebra. It also checks the leading-term
comparison used in Proposition~\ref{prop:injective}.
It checks the displayed
order-two, order-three and order-four formulas by exact
substitution, including the correspondence with the KS tables
of \citet{KudryashovZargaryan1996,ConteMusette2009},
the KS collision multiplicities, the
Kawahara coefficient restriction and its literature normalization,
the nodal correspondence, and the Fisher identity.
These finite substitutions check the displayed formulas; the uniform
proofs are the arguments in the text.
\item \texttt{p5\_count.sing} is a self-contained rational
\textsc{Singular} calculation for the full order-five matching ideal.
Its quotient dimension is 126.
Adjoining the matching Jacobian determinant gives the unit ideal,
so all 126 points are simple.
The input is reconstructed from the original polynomial sum by the
Python checker; the saved transcript states the two exact outputs.
\item \texttt{rigidity/} supplies the proof and exact certificate
for \((\mathrm R_d)\), \(d\le72\).
For \(2d+1=\ell^e+g\), \(g\in\{0,2,4,6\}\), the proof reduces the
normalized continuous convolution to a residual system of degree
at most \(g\).
The certificate retains every residual degree, with integer
ideal-membership identities and certified prime factors.
The replay reconstructs the inputs, multiplies out every identity,
and checks all 72 arithmetic choices.
It does not infer characteristic-zero nonexistence from a list
of residue-field points.
\item \texttt{symmetry\_checks/} contains the exact checks for
Sections~\ref{sec:reflection} and~\ref{sec:fixed}: the identities of
Proposition~\ref{prop:reflection} with symbolic coefficients for
\(d\le5\); the lengths \(F_p\) and the reducedness of
\(\Xp^{\mathrm{sym}}\) for \(p\le8\); the lengths \(2F_p\) of
Corollary~\ref{cor:elliptic-symmetric} for \(p\le6\) on a sample
lattice; the six pairs \eqref{eq:p5-symmetric}; and the scaling
\eqref{eq:fixed-wave} by substitution in the evolution equation.
\end{enumerate}

A Lean~4 formalization accompanies the paper at
\url{https://github.com/migita/binomial-count-meromorphic-waves},
directory \texttt{lean/}, with Lean and Mathlib pinned to
version~4.33.1. It proves \((\mathrm R_d)\) for \(2d+1\) prime
(\texttt{rigidity\_prime\_index}) and the count and simplicity
statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:prime}, both for the matching system
\eqref{eq:remainder} (\texttt{primeIndexCountAndSimplicity}) and for
the normalized pairs \((P,v)\) as solutions of \eqref{eq:main} in the
field of rational functions of \(e^{z}\)
(\texttt{primeIndexRationalExponentialCountAndSimplicity}), together
with the correspondence between the two formulations for every
\(p\ge2\). The discrete convolution is defined there by the
Bernoulli-polynomial power sums, and the remainder equations and their
Jacobian are those of Section~\ref{sec:counting}. The proof route is
described in Remark~\ref{rem:lean}. The files
\texttt{EnumerationCheck.lean} and \texttt{Check.lean} verify that the
proofs have exactly the stated propositions as their types and that the
theorems depend only on the axioms \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}; the project
contains no \texttt{sorry}, added axiom or \texttt{native\_decide}.

The scripts use exact rational or finite-field arithmetic.
The figure program evaluates the already verified formulas.
Instructions, dependency versions and checksums are included with the
source package. The prime-index theorem does not depend on the
computer calculations.
