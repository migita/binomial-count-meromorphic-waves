\section{Exact verification and ancillary material}\label{app:verification}

This appendix describes the computer checks that accompany the paper.
The scripts use exact rational or finite-field arithmetic;
instructions, dependency versions and checksums are included with the
source package. The prime-index theorem does not depend on the computer
calculations. The ancillary material contains five kinds of checks.
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item \texttt{verify\_manuscript.py} reconstructs the discrete and
continuous convolutions, the low-order Laurent coefficients, and the
Weierstrass differential algebra. It checks the leading-term comparison
used in Proposition~\ref{prop:injective}, and the displayed order-two,
order-three and order-four formulas by exact substitution, including
the correspondence of Table~\ref{tab:ks} with the tables of
\citet{KudryashovZargaryan1996,ConteMusette2009}, the multiplicities
\eqref{eq:ks-collisions}, the Kawahara coefficient restriction and its
literature normalization, the nodal correspondence, and the Fisher
identity. These finite substitutions check the displayed formulas; the
uniform proofs are the arguments in the text.
\item \texttt{p5\_count.sing} is a self-contained \textsc{Singular}
calculation over \(\QQ\) for the full matching equations at \(p=5\).
Its quotient dimension, which is the count with multiplicity, is
\(126\). Adjoining the Jacobian determinant gives the unit ideal: the
determinant vanishes at no solution, so all \(126\) solutions are
simple.
\item At \(p=8\) the same two outputs, \(6435\) and the unit ideal, are
obtained over the finite field \(\FF_{32003}\). Every solution modulo
\(32003\) is therefore simple and lifts uniquely by Hensel's lemma, as
in Step~4 of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:prime}, and the lifts are
all the solutions because the count with multiplicity is \(6435\), by
Proposition~\ref{prop:general}(ii). At \(p=11\) each of the
\(352716\) solutions is certified over the \(19\)-adic numbers, by
Hensel's lemma at the simple points of the reduction modulo \(19\) and
by exact Newton-polygon and Newton--Kantorovich certificates at the
singular ones. The archived certificate and the record of its
validation are in the repository, directory \texttt{certificates/}.
\item \texttt{rigidity/} supplies the proof and exact certificate for
rigidity \((\mathrm R_d)\), \(d=p-1\le72\). For \(2d+1=\ell^e+g\),
\(g\in\{0,2,4,6\}\), the proof reduces the normalized continuous
convolution to a residual system of degree at most \(g\); the case
\(g=0\), a prime power, is also proved in the companion note
(Section~\ref{sec:further}). The certificate retains every residual
degree, with integer ideal-membership identities and certified prime
factors; the replay reconstructs the inputs, multiplies out every
identity and checks all 72 arithmetic choices. It does not infer
characteristic-zero nonexistence from a list of residue-field points.
\item \texttt{symmetry\_checks/} contains the exact checks for the two
pure subfamilies and for single equations: the identities of
Proposition~\ref{prop:reflection} with symbolic coefficients for
\(d\le5\); the labels of Table~\ref{tab:ks} and of the \(21\) real
pairs at \(p=4\), with the statements of
Proposition~\ref{cor:endpoints} and Theorem~\ref{thm:symmetric}(ii)
about them; the counts with multiplicity \(F_p\) for \(p\le8\), and
finite-field certificates, of the same kind as at \(p=8\), that the
\(F_p\) pairs are distinct for every \(p\le14\); on a sample lattice,
the counts with multiplicity \(2F_p\) of
Corollary~\ref{cor:elliptic-symmetric} for \(p\le6\), and the absence
of elliptic pairs of purely dissipative equations for \(p=3,5,7\)
(\texttt{elliptic\_dissipative.py}, Section~\ref{sec:reflection}); the
six pairs \eqref{eq:p5-symmetric} and the ten pairs
\eqref{eq:p6-eliminant}, with the sextic factor in full; and the
scaling \eqref{eq:fixed-wave} by substitution in the evolution equation.
\end{enumerate}

A Lean~4 formalization accompanies the paper at
\url{https://github.com/migita/binomial-count-meromorphic-waves},
directory \texttt{lean/}, with Lean and Mathlib pinned to
version~4.33.1. It proves rigidity \((\mathrm R_d)\) for \(2d+1\) prime
(\texttt{rigidity\_prime\_index}) and the count and simplicity
statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:prime}, both for the matching equations
\eqref{eq:remainder} (\texttt{primeIndexCountAndSimplicity}) and for
the normalized pairs \((P,v)\) as solutions of \eqref{eq:main} in the
field of rational functions of \(e^{z}\)
(\texttt{primeIndexRationalExponentialCountAndSimplicity}), together
with the correspondence between the two formulations for every
\(p\ge2\). The discrete convolution is defined there by the
Bernoulli-polynomial power sums; the remainder equations and their
Jacobian are those of Section~\ref{sec:counting}, and the proof route
is that of Remark~\ref{rem:lean}. The files
\texttt{EnumerationCheck.lean} and \texttt{Check.lean} verify that the
proofs have exactly the stated propositions as their types and that the
theorems depend only on the axioms \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}; the project
contains no \texttt{sorry}, added axiom or \texttt{native\_decide}.
